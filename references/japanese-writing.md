# Japanese writing review

Use this when drafting or editing reader-facing Japanese copy.

- Preserve the writer's vocabulary, level of formality, facts, dates, numbers,
  quotations, and degree of certainty. Do not invent personal experience or
  turn an example into a verified event.
- Answer the reader's concrete concern near the start. Use specific actors,
  objects, conditions, and actions where an abstract sentence is hard to follow.
- Remove repeated introductions, previews, conclusions, and claims about the
  writer's sincerity when they add no article-specific information.
- Revise only passages that need it. Prefer deletion over rewriting a sound
  paragraph. Do not mechanically vary sentence endings or add casual errors.
- Allow ordinary scenes, hypothetical examples, opinions, and humor when they
  suit the article. Check externally verifiable claims against relevant sources.
- Read the revised text against the original for meaning, certainty, voice,
  and repetition. For an edit request, return the edited copy; explain only
  changes that affect meaning or that the user asks about.
